# DECISIONS — Gisement

Numbered decisions that change or clarify the spec. A lot never diverges
silently: it adds a decision here.

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | 2026-11-11 | The ineliminable share is computed on **gross** time (`temps_brut × part_atypique`), not on the fragmented time as §13.2 writes it. | Annex A.3 is the acceptance reference (R2: 18.4 net hours, 772.80 €/month, 13.7 months, factor 2.61). With §13.2 read literally the same case gives 20.56 h, 863.52 € and a factor of 2.34 — every published figure of the spec (KPI-4, §13.3, annexes A, D, E, G.5, video 1:32) would be wrong. The reference case wins over the formula line. |
| D-02 | 2026-11-11 | Monte-Carlo spreads are **derived from measurement quality** (standard error over the window for the occurrences, `σ/√n` for a measured duration, ±0.05 band for a fragmentation coefficient, ±0.05 for a review rate, 0.02 for an atypical share measured on ≥ 100 cases). | Annex A.6 lists wide distributions (occurrences σ = 42, fragmentation U(0.60, 0.80)) and then publishes a narrow interval [14.8 – 22.1]. The two are arithmetically incompatible: those distributions give σ ≈ 7 h, i.e. ±11.6 h at 90 %. Deriving the spread from the number of observed cases reproduces the published interval (±3.7 h) and is the honest model — a duration measured on 1 840 cases is not uncertain by ±20 %. Point values (R2) and determinism (R4) stay authoritative. |
| D-03 | 2026-11-11 | `.mergify.yml` is committed, but the Merge Queue **product is not activated** (it needs a dashboard click or an admin token, which this machine does not have). Lots are integrated wave by wave, CI green on `main` before the next wave starts. | Mergify stays the only merge pilot for every PR opened from now on; the queue product must be activated before submission week (spec §29). Until then a PR is merged by the maintainer with `gh pr merge --rebase --delete-branch`, never by hand on the website. |
| D-04 | 2026-11-11 | Data files are **JSON**, not YAML. | No PyYAML in the CI job: the 5-minute budget (user rule 2026-10-24) must not be spent installing a parser for configuration data. JSON is also exactly what DynamoDB and Amazon Quick expect. |
| D-05 | 2026-11-11 | CI runs on `ubuntu-latest`, overridable with the repository variable `CI_RUNNER`. | The r630 self-hosted fleet factory refuses to register runners above 90 % disk and the box is at 93 %. A private repository gets 2 000 hosted minutes a month, which covers the whole hackathon at ~2 min per run. Switching to `["self-hosted","gisement-worker"]` is one variable change. |
| D-06 | 2026-11-11 | The frozen vocabulary is a **package** `src/gisement/types/` (communs, campagne, mesure, economie, conformite), re-exported by `types/__init__.py`. | A single `types.py` holding 25 dataclasses and 13 enumerations would break the 500-line rule (ENF-15) on day one. Lots import `from gisement.types import ...` and never see the split. |
| D-07 | 2026-11-11 | The model interface (`Modele`, `ReponseModele`, `ModeleFictif`) is delivered by lot 0. | Lots 10 (classifier) and 16 (writer) both need a model and must not block on each other. The fake is deterministic, accounts for its tokens, and is the only model any test ever instantiates (§26.4). |
| D-08 | 2026-11-11 | Identifiers in code are ASCII; domain words stay in French; every deliverable of the submission is in English (ENF-14). | Accented constants are a typo magnet across 25 lots written by different contributors. The French domain vocabulary is kept because the client-facing reports are French first (§12.7 EF-65). |
| D-09 | 2026-11-11 | The AgentCore Policy document is **generated from** the local reference engine (`politique/agentcore.py`), which is what enforces the rules in the deployed gateway middleware. | Point 5 of spec chapter 34 (the real syntax of an AgentCore Policy refusal based on a group size) is unconfirmed at S0. Generating the artefact from the engine means the two cannot drift, and R5 to R7 are testable today, offline, without waiting for the J+5 test. |
| D-11 | 2026-11-11 | `EntreeGisement` carries `part_volume_traitee` and `suppression_etape`; a type A action that removes a step is computed without transfer and without ineliminable share. | Annex A.7 publishes 1 102 €/month for moving 78 % of the supplier orders to the EDI portal, which is `250 × 9 min × 0.70 × 42 €` with **no** transfer and **no** ineliminable correction. Without these two fields the engine could not reproduce either the annex A.3 case (the B variant) or the annex A.7 case (the A action) — and the A action is the very recommendation that makes the report credible. |
| D-12 | 2026-11-11 | Wave 1 may hold up to **three** projects (`config/priorite.json`). | §15.3 writes "1 to 2 projects" while annex E, the reference portfolio, publishes three (P-14, P-07, P-03 for a total of 8 400 €). The budget ceiling of 15 000 € is the real constraint; the count follows the worked example. |
| D-13 | 2026-11-11 | §28.1's "monthly group reporting, 1/month, category E" is process **P-22** (board reporting), and **P-19** is the monthly management reporting that annex E puts in wave 2. | The two rows of the spec contradict each other on the same name. Splitting them keeps annex E (the portfolio) and §28.1 (the trap list) both true. |
| D-14 | 2026-11-11 | The occurrences uncertainty of the Monte-Carlo is `ecart_type / sqrt(nb_mois)`; the published seasonal amplitude may come from the weekly extremes x 4.33. | Annex A.1 publishes a 210-465 amplitude **and** a standard deviation of 42 on six monthly counts. Six values spanning 255 cannot have a standard deviation of 42. Read as a weekly amplitude extrapolated to a month, both figures hold and the interval of annex A.6 ([14.8 - 22.1]) is reproduced. |
| D-15 | 2026-11-11 | The demo portfolio publishes **6** advised-against processes (26 %), not the 5 (22 %) of annex E. | P-02 carries 55 % atypical cases, which mechanically triggers the judgement threshold of 16.1 (> 40 %). Annex F.3 explicitly accepts "D or E" for P-02; a rule that bends for the example is not a rule. 26 % stays inside the 15-30 % band of KPI-9 and R16. |
| D-16 | 2026-11-11 | Projects that do not fit the wave caps are published in `Portefeuille.reserve`, ranked and scored. | 17 of the 23 demo processes are actionable; 15.3 caps the waves at 1-3, 2-4 and up to 6 projects. Annex E itself leaves eleven processes out of its three waves. Hiding them would lose the point of a portfolio; a ranked reserve keeps it honest. |
| D-18 | 2026-11-11 | Transfer rates are drawn in a **relative** band (+/-25 % of the rate), not an absolute one. | Annex A.6 draws a 20 % review rate in [15 %, 25 %]. An absolute +/-5 points band applied to a 5 % reject rate would mean "between zero and double", which inflates the published interval to [13.9 - 23.1] instead of the [14.8 - 22.1] the spec publishes. The relative band reproduces annex A.6 for the review rate and stays honest for the small ones. |
| D-17 | 2026-11-11 | Payback delays in months round **half to even**; hours and euros still round half up. | Annex A.2 publishes `10 584 / 2 016 = 5.2 months`: the exact quotient is 5.25. Half-up would print 5.3 and break the naive/real table that the executive report prints for every process (R2, KPI-4). |
| D-10 | 2026-11-11 | One campaign = one seed, stored in `Campagne.graine`, used by the email sampling, the Monte-Carlo and the demo generator. | ENF-08 (reproducibility) is only provable if a single seed drives every random step; the seed is published in the report. |
