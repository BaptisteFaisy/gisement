Motivation The teams that operate a Kafka cluster have no direct way to see how the clients connected to it are configured. In many organizations, the teams developing and deploying client applications are separate and intentionally siloed from the teams that manage the cluster (the most extreme case being management via a hosted service). As a result, an operator investigating a problem has to get the configuration from each application team, which delays the diagnosis and troubleshooting. The gap matters because client configuration is a common source of problems. The large number of available configuration options bewilders teams new to the ecosystem, and even accomplished teams are not immune to the occasional misconfiguration. The gap impact gets more noticeable as Kafka’s usage increases within organizations, so centralized observability of client configuration becomes increasingly critical.
